One in four recognized pregnancies ends in loss, and yet most women who experience it describe the same thing: a grief with nowhere obvious to go. No funeral, often no name spoken aloud, a return to work within days as though nothing had happened.

That is beginning to change, unevenly. Support groups are forming in church basements and hospital hallways. A small number of employers now recognize miscarriage leave explicitly. Doctors are being trained to say the loss out loud instead of stepping around it.

What women who have lived through it ask for is rarely complicated: to have the loss acknowledged as real, and to not be asked, six weeks later, why they still seem sad.
